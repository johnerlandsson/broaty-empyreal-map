# The walked land

*Status: built. An octagon each walked place at its true spot, routes, notes and forgetting, and
Tamsin from above in the top band. Last updated 2026-11-20. Design:
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-13-land-automap-design.md` (decision 17 is this layout); the picked
looks: `reference/land-picked.png` for the octagons, grounds and tracks, and option B of
`reference/land-ways-options.png` for where they sit, and E of
`reference/land-curves-lit-options.png` for the ways between them (decision 19), and B of
`reference/land-spread-options.png` for the spread (decision 20), and A of
`reference/land-climbs-options.png` for climbs and game trails (decision 21).*

## What it is and when it shows

Since empyreal b6728d0 you can walk out of Zennor onto Tamsin's generated land, 30 km round the
city. There is no plan of it in the game's data, so the map draws only the places you have walked,
as you walk them, and keeps them per character across logouts.

It shows whenever the location's area starts with `surface.` (`ui/lib/land-choose.js`,
`landShown`): since empyreal's all-worlds work every solid world's land is open, and you can set a
ship down anywhere and walk. It shows too in a site's rooms (area `site.<id>`), on the land of the
site's walked front (`siteLand`; see "Sites" below). Both bands are the land's then: the station models, decks,
ships and cars are hidden, and the info line gives the place's name as the game sends it ("Old
Shaft on the Terrace Track"). Stepping back into Zennor brings Zennor's skyline and plan back. The
chart, the lanes map and the belt map still cover the top band as they do anywhere.

Only walked places are ever drawn or named. A way you haven't taken is a stub, and where it goes is
never named. No height, distance in km or other number about the ground is shown; the only number
is a route's "N steps the way you know".

## Where its data comes from

- **The game.** Each `mudstd.room.info` frame in a `surface.*` area is posted by the Lua side
  (`src/station_map/land.lua`, `src/main.lua`) as a `land-room` message after `location`: `id`,
  `name`, `area`, `areaName`, `terrain`, `coords` (km east `x` and north `y` of the land's port,
  Zennor's point on Tamsin, or of 0° N 0° E on a world with no port; height `z`), `wrap` (the km
  round the world at which `x` comes round, gmcp.md §9.5) and `exits` (`{ id, area?, inverse?, style? }` by compass point; `style` is how the way goes,
  `climb`, `road` or `trail`, empyreal `docs/gmcp.md` §9.5.3). An exit into Zennor carries
  `area: "zennor"`. The game sends no chart of the land and never names a place you haven't
  walked.
- **The kind of ground** is the place's `terrain` (empyreal `docs/gmcp.md` §9.5.1), one of eleven:
  water, ice, woods, reeds, scrub, cushions, salt, rock, stones, dust, earth. The store keeps it on
  the place and in the saved record. A place saved before the game sent it, or sent as `room` (its
  tile not built yet; a later `room` keeps a terrain already known), falls back to its name, which
  the game builds from a fixed list (`placeTitle()` in empyreal `surface-words.ts`):
  `ui/lib/land-kinds.js` maps a name to one of thirteen ground groups: twelve from the title (open,
  waymark, high, slope, low, rock, dust, workings, water, salt, ice, molten) and road for the road
  ends (`….join.…` ids). `styleOf()` picks the colour and texture from both; a road end and the
  Edge of the Lava (sent as `rock`) keep their group over any terrain, since the terrain can't say
  them. The store keeps any terrain but `room` as sent, so one the board can't draw yet (drawn by
  its title meanwhile) is there once it can. "on the X Track" puts a place on track
  X; "on the Downs", "by the Carn" give its region.
- **Zennor's block and roads out** come from `ui/data/land.js`, generated by `npm run build:data`
  from `content/game/land.yaml`: the port it's measured `from`, the `town` outline in km and each
  road end's spot and Zennor room (`joins`). Nothing else of land.yaml is written, since its
  heights, valleys and tracks would show land you haven't walked. A world with land but no entry
  still draws its walked places, with no town (`landWorld`).
- **The globe** is the world's face (`faceOf(<body>)`, `ui/lib/worlds.js`; plain grey for a world
  with none) turned to the latitude of its land's port (`from`) in `ui/data/sky.js`, else 0
  (`landLatitude`).

## Away from Zennor

- **A world with no port** (empyreal `docs/all-worlds.md` §6) has the same area `surface.<world>`
  and ids, with coords from 0° N 0° E, so thousands of km out. It draws its walked places as
  anywhere, with no town, no roads and no way-home card (`landWorld` gives it no `town`).
- **The seam.** The game's `x` jumps by the `wrap` at 180° from where the land is measured, which on
  a world with no port is 180° E/W, where sites stand (the Lighter on Carrow, at 178.8° W). The
  layout draws each place on the side of the seam nearest where you stand (`layoutLand`'s `near`),
  so places across it sit side by side.
- **Orrin's town** is named for the port its land is measured from (`townName`: Dolcoath), on its
  block and its edge card, not Zennor.
- **The globe** moves your spot by your coords within a port's land (100 km, the game's figure);
  past it, and anywhere on a world with no port, it turns with you, so your spot stays where the
  land's edge would put it, toward you, a few px from a little north of the middle, never jumping
  (its facets aren't the world's geography).

## Sites

A site (empyreal `docs/set-pieces.md` §3: a hut, a wreck, a camp) is a **front**, a place of the land
like any other (`surface.<world>.site.<id>`, with coords and terrain), and up to six roofed rooms
behind it (`site.<id>.<room>`, area `site.<id>`) through compass doors from the front. The game sends
the rooms with no coords (it means to send the front's, set-pieces.md decision 40, not built yet).

- Lua passes a site's room as a `land-room` with `site` (its id) and no `coords`.
- The store keeps each walked room by its front (`rooms()`, `room(id)`, `standing(id)`), in the saved
  record's `rooms`, never as a place of the land. A room is taken only once its front is walked; it
  goes with its front's land on **Forget this land…** (the room you stand in stays, its front too).
  Routes run through rooms as standing at their front, so a route from inside a hut leads out of it,
  and its steps count the rooms.
- **Rooms at their doors** (A of `reference/land-sites-options.png`, picked 2026-11-25; as built,
  `reference/land-sites-built.png`). Each room you've entered is a 0.5 km roofed square, dark with a
  solid Stone edge as Zennor's block is, just outside the front's face its door leaves by (its middle
  0.42 km out), joined to the face by a short Stone door. A room through another room's compass door
  is a step on (0.62 km along each axis it names); one by up, down, in or out sits in the first free
  spot beside the room it's reached from, clockwise from straight out from the front. Rooms never
  overlap each other or an octagon; one with no free spot isn't drawn (`layoutLand`'s `rooms` and
  `doors`). A door not gone through is a short Dim bar out of the face: there's a way in, never
  where to, and the room behind it isn't named.
- Inside, your room's square glows amber as your octagon does (the glow on the square itself:
  WebKit draws nothing for a group that small under the glow filter), with no ways lit, since you
  can't leave a room by the front's. Hover a room for its name; click it for the route there, which
  runs through the rooms' squares; right-clicking a room opens no menu (no note, and not the
  ground's forgetting). A front's door into a room drawn by another front, or with no free spot,
  is a short Stone door. Logging in inside a room, the land shows once the saved map (with its
  front) has loaded.

## What's kept

`ui/lib/land-store.js` (`LandStore`) keeps the walked places: each one's `name`, `x`, `y`, `z`,
exits (each `{ to, area?, back?, style? }`, any style kept as sent) and `note`, in the order first walked. Routes run on a copy of the Mallard automapper's graph
and route finder in `ui/lib/automap/` (see its README for the commit and our changes). A step into a place walked before, just as it was, only moves where you are: the store's
`version` (which counts changes to the places, exits and notes) stays, nothing is saved, the routing
graph is kept, and the board moves only your light, the route and the cards.

The panel posts the store's record as `land-save` `{ who, record }`, debounced 1 s, and never while
no character is known; the waiting save is also flushed when the panel is hidden or closed. The Lua
side writes it under `land:<who>` in Mallard's storage, for whichever character it's sent for (the
panel only ever sends a character's own map).

Lua keeps whose map the panel has (`land_who`, apart from the lanes' character). When `Char.Status`
names someone else, or anyone at all after a disconnect or a logout, Lua sends her map as `land-load`
`{ who, record }`, then the last land frame again: on login the room frame comes before the status,
so it was walked into no one's map. The panel first saves the one before's waiting change under her
name, then loads; places walked while no one's map was loaded are merged in, the newer winning. A
disconnect, or `Char.Logout` (`logout`/`menu` goes back to the character menu on the same
connection, with no disconnect), sends `land-load` with no `who`: the panel saves what's waiting and
clears the map, its notes and where you are. The last land frame is forgotten then, and whenever a
room frame off the land comes, so it's never replayed for anyone after. When the panel opens, Lua
replays the map's `land-load` and the last land frame, if you're still on the land.
A missing or bad stored value loads as an empty map.

## Drawing the board

`ui/land-deck.js` draws the bottom band; `ui/lib/land-layout.js` works out the layout.

- **True spots, spread out.** A regular octagon, 0.96 km flat to flat with its faces to the eight
  compass points, sits where each walked place really is (its `x`, `y`, km east and north of
  Zennor's point), with the whole land, Zennor's block too, drawn 1.3× further from Zennor's point
  than it is (`SPREAD`). The land keeps its shape, and close places (the game puts them at least
  1.1 km apart) get room between them: at least 1.43 km on the map, so a gap of about half a km
  between their octagons. Nothing is snapped to a grid: the first build put each place in a
  tile of a 1.2 km board, but off the tracks places sit about 2.2 km apart, so most ways joined
  octagons that weren't neighbours, and one in four neighbours had snapped to the wrong side of
  its exit (`reference/land-ways-options.png`); a later try with a 1.2 km grid was taken back
  (`reference/land-grid-options.png`).
- **Ground.** Each octagon has its terrain's (else its group's) colour with its texture over it,
  in screen pixels, so the texture is the same size at any zoom. The terrains are the game's hues
  brought down to the board's dark earth. Where the title says the place's shape or a thing on it
  (high, slope, low, waymark, workings), that texture goes over the terrain's colour; else the
  ground's own (`reference/land-terrain-options.png`, B: A was picked 2026-11-19, then B on review
  the next day so the lie of the land still shows). The colours are in the style guide's §4.2
  ("On the land's map").
- **Ways.** Every way is one thin Earth curve drawn under the octagons, from the middle of the face
  its exit names to the middle of the face of the exit back, square to both: it's pulled out of
  each face by 0.42 of the gap between them (at least 0.12 km, at most 0.75). The game names an
  exit by the true bearing, so the curve hardly turns, and nothing has a corner. A one-way way
  (down a scree) curves from its face into the other place's middle and gets a small Fixture-line
  chevron in the gap, pointing the way you can go, the same size at any zoom.
- **Your exits lit.** The ways you can leave your place by are the same curves drawn lighter
  (`#7d6b55`) and at a heavier weight, so the sides you can go by show at a glance. A one-way way
  into your place isn't lit, and a track isn't either. The route follows the ways' curves.
- **Climbs and game trails** (A of `reference/land-climbs-options.png`, decision 21). A way either
  end's exit calls a `climb` (a scramble, scree or thick growth: it tires you) is its curve in
  `#5a4a38` with short ticks across it, 1.4 px every 6 px, 3 px either side of the line; one
  called a `trail` (a game trail) is a line of `#5a4a38` dots 4 px apart. A climb wins over a road
  and a road over a trail, as the game's own order has it. Out of your place both are lit as
  any way. A way not taken that the game calls a climb has its dotted stub ticked too. A style the
  board doesn't know is a plain way.
- **Tracks** are the ways either end calls a `road`; where neither end sent a style (a place
  saved before the game sent them), the ways the names put on one track. They're drawn as
  Zennor's roads, straight from middle to middle over the octagons they run through: an Earth
  band with a dashed Stone edge, named in Dim italic beside the walked track (by its places'
  names; a road none of them names goes unnamed), clear of its octagons. Region names sit in Dim italic among their walked places.
- **Zennor** is a dark block with a dashed Stone edge and "ZENNOR", with an Earth road to each road
  end you've walked. An exit into Zennor is that road, never a stub.
- **Ways not taken** are short dotted Dim stubs out of the face they leave by.
- **Your place** glows amber, as on every plan. A place with a note has a small folded corner.
- **No hachures.** Heights come to 0.1 km, too coarse to tell a climb from level ground; the high,
  slope and low textures carry the lie of the land.
- **Chrome.** `N ▲`, + − ⌂, the wheel and drag as on every plan. It starts centred on you at about
  26 px a km and re-centres only when you walk out of the middle of the view; ⌂ centres on you.

## Finding your way

- **Click a walked place** to light the shortest walked route to it from where you are: a dashed
  amber line with no glow, a dashed amber outline on the place, and a card with its name and "N
  steps the way you know". Click it again, click empty ground or press Esc to clear. Walking on
  keeps the target and redraws the route, until you arrive.
- **The way home.** When the town (Zennor, Dolcoath) is wholly off the view, a "← Zennor" card sits
  on the edge toward it; clicking it lights the route to the nearest walked road end. A world with no
  port has none.
- **Hover** a walked place for its name and note.

## Notes and forgetting

Right-click a walked place for **Note…** and **Remove note**. Right-click empty ground for
**Forget this land…**, which asks once, then clears this character's map of this world's land
(the place you stand in stays). Notes are kept with the
map.

## The top band

`ui/land-globe.js`: Tamsin from above, a whole low-poly globe on the seeded stars in its own
colours, lit from a fixed light at the upper left with no day and night. Your spot is amber with
its ring and the glow, a little north of the middle at Zennor's latitude, moved by your `coords`
(at most 30 km, so in practice it's Zennor's spot). No labels and no weather.

## Wiring

`ui/main.js` builds one `LandStore` for every world's land (a place's id carries its body,
`surface.<body>.…`), and a `LandDeck` and `LandGlobe` for each world on its first land location
(`landBody`). Each deck draws only its own world's places. `land-room` goes into the store, then
that world's board redraws and follows you and its globe moves your spot; `land-load` loads the
store and redraws. `render()` takes the land branch before the stations': it hides the station
model and every deck, shows the current world's views (hiding every other world's) and sets the
info line; leaving the land hides them again.

## Keeping up with the game

- `ui/lib/land-kinds.test.js` ("the game's titles"), run when `~/dev/empyreal` is there, collects
  every title `placeTitle` can return and fails if one isn't in `land-kinds.js`'s table. See
  [sync.md](sync.md) for what to do then.
- Land on a new world (`content/game/land.yaml`) is an area `surface.<world>`, which maps with no
  change; `build:data` adds its `town` and `joins` to `ui/data/land.js`.
- Updating the copied engine: `ui/lib/automap/README.md`.
