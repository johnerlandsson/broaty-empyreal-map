A giant from high orbit, by its envelope

Every gaseous world is the same ochre today. Each now authors its gases, albedo and heat from inside (planets.md §3.2), and the game works out its temperature at 1 bar from them. Here a world's cloud deck is whichever of its gases the game's phaseOf has frozen or liquid at that temperature: ammonia on the four cold Jupiters (cream and tan, Jupiter-like), water on the cool sub-Neptunes (white), none on the warm ones, the ice giants and Caradon 1 (clear gas, seen deep). Methane over it takes out red light, blue-green by its share and half as much over a deck, which sends light back from higher up (2% on ice giants and sub-Neptunes, 0.2% on the Jupiters). Albedo says how bright: Caradon 1's 0.1 is dark. A locked world whose night side is past the Draper point glows there: Caradon 1's 1,000 K, dull red. All under its star's light at .45, low-poly facets, bands from giant option B.
